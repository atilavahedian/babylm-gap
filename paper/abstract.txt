A language model trained on 10M words falls 0.715 nats/word (calibrated) short of a model of the same size trained on 100M words. We ask where that gap lives and how much of it a learner that sees only 10M words could close under the BabyLM 2025 strict-small rules. Using seed ensembles and a distillation oracle on the same held-out words, we split the raw gap between 10-epoch 10M models and the 100M model, 0.74 nats/word, into single-model noise (0.29, the gain of an 8-model ensemble), an upper bound on structure that is in the 10M words but unlearned (0.23), and a lower bound on information that 10M words do not contain (0.22). A perfect legal learner could therefore gain at most about 4x in data-equivalent terms, and 10x is not in the data. The 100M model was not tuned at 100M; a better one would widen the gap, raising the absent part and lowering the ceiling. We then report ten pre-registered attacks on the gap, all of which failed, and one measured point: a two-model ensemble, a known technique, beats the best single model of equal compute by 0.086 nats/word at 28 layers (at least 1.22x data-equivalent), and the gain holds across two sizes. On the official BabyLM zero-shot suite, however, this gain does not clear the seed-noise bar, and our best model scores below the published GPT-BERT causal-focus baseline (38.97 vs 40.32 on the composite with reading rescored). All bars were registered before the results they judged, except two disclosed rules that only tightened them.
